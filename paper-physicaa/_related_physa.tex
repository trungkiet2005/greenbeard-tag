\subsection{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Tags, greenbeards and population structure.}
Evolutionary game dynamics describes a population of competing designs as a
nonlinear flow on a simplex, so a change in cooperation is a change of
stability and basin structure rather than only of an average
payoff~\citep{taylor1978ess,hofbauer1998evolutionary,hofbauer2003evolutionary,sandholm2010population}.
Statistical-physics work on social dilemmas shows that these transitions depend
strongly on interaction structure~\citep{perc2017statphys}: spatial contact,
degree heterogeneity and multilayer coupling each stabilise
cooperation~\citep{nowak1992spatial,santos2005scale,wang2015multilayer}, and
conditional strategies change which regime is reached~\citep{szolnoki2012conditional}.
Recent work in this journal treats reputation, trust thresholds and social
exclusion in the same way, as mechanisms that reorganise cooperative regimes
rather than as parameters that shift a
mean~\citep{quan2019exclusion,zhu2025adaptive,zhang2026trust}. Tags are one
route to assortment without memory, as phenotypic similarity in a well-mixed
population~\citep{antal2009phenotypic} and as a mutating trait in a finite
one~\citep{traulsen2007chromodynamics,traulsen2003minimal}. The biological
origin is the greenbeard: a marker and the behaviour it elicits are inherited
together~\citep{hamilton1964genetical1,dawkins1976selfish}. Tag similarity can
sustain cooperation~\citep{riolo2001evolution}, but falsebeards profit once the
marker separates from the behaviour~\citep{roberts2002does,jansen2006altruism,gardner2010greenbeards},
and tags plus a harmful conduct produce
evilbeards~\citep{garcia2014evilbeards}. Our tag differs from all of these in
that its reliability is a controllable property of a verification technology
rather than a fixed or accidental one, and in that its failure changes which
face of the simplex attracts the flow. The distinction is not cosmetic. Where
those models let a marker come apart from its behaviour either as symmetric
recognition noise or as a discrete impostor lineage, a forged credential is a
continuous, adversarial and one-sided parameter, and it is the one-sidedness
that reverses the order in which the regime fails. We study the same object in
both the deterministic replicator flow and its finite-population birth-death
counterpart~\citep{traulsen2006stochastic}, and report where the two readings
disagree rather than presenting either as the model.

\paragraph{Parochial altruism.}
Models of ethnocentrism and parochial altruism also pair an in-group tag with
distinct out-group conduct~\citep{hammond2006ethnocentrism,choi2007parochial}.
They rely on unforgeable or jointly inherited tags, on spatial structure, or on
competition between groups. Here the tag can be forged at an exogenous pass
rate, and out-group harshness is selected because it makes costly membership
excludable, not because groups fight. There is no selection above the level of
the individual design anywhere in the model.

\paragraph{Signalling and certification.}
The credential is also a secret handshake: a pre-play signal a mutant may
display without the conduct it advertises~\citep{robson1990efficiency}.
Classical signalling models keep signals honest through differential
costs~\citep{grafen1990biological,spence1973job}, and certification
intermediaries instead disclose otherwise hidden
quality~\citep{dranove2010quality,leland1979quacks}. Our forger pays strictly
less than a compliant member, so honesty cannot rest on a realised cost and
rests on imperfect detection and on the off-equilibrium cost of being
caught~\citep{szamado2011cost}. Penalties and detection are ordinarily
substitutes~\citep{becker1968crime}; the model identifies where that
substitution fails, which is where detection is fixed by cryptography rather
than by an inspection budget.

\paragraph{AI races, agent identity and what is missing.}
Evolutionary models of AI development races have studied regulation, voluntary
commitments, auditing and
trust~\citep{han2020regulate,han2022voluntary,bova2024vigilant}, and we take the
reduced four-strategy race of~\citet{domingos2026falling} as an unmodified
interaction layer so that every effect reported here is attributable to the
identity layer above it. Agent-governance work treats identity, visibility and
provenance as prerequisites for any of
this~\citep{chan2024visibility,chan2025infrastructure,otsuka2026aiidentity,kolt2025governing},
while empirical work documents identity-conditioned evaluation and imperfect
recognition of model
origin~\citep{panickssery2024llm,sun2025idiosyncrasies,wang2026outgroup}. What
neither literature supplies is the population-level consequence of a credential
that is simultaneously valuable, costly and worth forging. Supplying it is the
purpose of this paper, through closed-form invasion boundaries, an exact
reduction on the invariant faces, and a global regime map.
