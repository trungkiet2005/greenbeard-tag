\begin{abstract}
Machine agents increasingly carry identity credentials, and a credential that
changes how its bearers are treated is a greenbeard whose classical weakness is
that an impostor can wear it. For software the marker separates from the
behaviour at a rate the verification technology sets, so forgery is continuous,
adversarial and one-sided rather than symmetric noise. We attach genuine,
forged or absent badges and badge-contingent conduct to an unmodified
four-strategy development race, giving 48 designs on a 47-dimensional simplex
under replicator dynamics. The central result is a reduction rather than a
measurement: the fully badged and fully unbadged faces of the simplex carry the
same four-conduct replicator flow, differing only by the constant certification
cost. Their rest points, stability and attractors coincide, so a settled
certified market carries exactly the harm of an uncertified one and is poorer
by its dues, at every verification quality, fine, liability and assortment.
Neither the forgery rate nor the fine appears in the reduced payoff, so the
entire verification layer is invisible from inside a settled regime. What
forgeability determines is composition rather than conduct: a behavioural mimic
invades before an outright exploiter, so the credential empties while conduct
still looks sound. The exclusion that sustains a credential is a dial and not a
switch, an entry toll of $2.0$ already delivering $72\%$ of the forgery
tolerance that a toll of $30.4$ buys.
\end{abstract}
