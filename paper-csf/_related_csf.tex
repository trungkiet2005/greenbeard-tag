\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Evolutionary dynamics as a nonlinear system.}
The formal setting is evolutionary game
dynamics~\citep{taylor1978ess,hofbauer1998evolutionary,hofbauer2003evolutionary}:
a population of competing designs is a nonlinear flow on a simplex, qualitative
changes of behaviour are bifurcations of that flow, and the objects of interest
are collective rather than
individual~\citep{perc2010coevolutionary,perc2017statphys}. That programme has
established that transitions between cooperative and defecting phases are sharp
and often reducible to a universal
form~\citep{perc2016phase,wang2015universal}, and that much of the outcome is
decided by who meets whom, through networks, layers or
conformity~\citep{nowak1992spatial,santos2005scale,ohtsuki2006simple,szabo2007evolutionary,wang2015multilayer,szolnoki2015conformity}.
Tags have been studied there as generators of assortment without memory, as
phenotypic similarity in well-mixed populations~\citep{antal2009phenotypic}
and as a mutating tag space in finite
ones~\citep{traulsen2007chromodynamics}. Two features separate this
system. First, the tag is forgeable at a rate the modeller sets, so the
bifurcation parameter belongs to a verification technology rather than to the
players, and the comparative statics are with respect to an engineering budget.
Second, the flow carries two disjoint attracting faces differing in whether the
tag exists at all rather than in how much cooperation it supports, so what
matters is basin structure and not equilibrium level. Whether either regime
arises at all turns on a classical condition, that altruism invades only under
positive assortment~\citep{eshel1982assortment,fletcher2009simple}, supplied
here by one provider's agents sharing a platform; by
Proposition~\ref{prop:futility} the badge contributes nothing to nucleation, so
assortment is the only route.

\paragraph{Greenbeards, forgeable tags and parochial conduct.}
Kin selection~\citep{hamilton1964genetical1,hamilton1964genetical2} explains
why a gene favours copies of itself in others, and a greenbeard is a gene
carrying both marker and
behaviour~\citep{dawkins1976selfish}. \citet{riolo2001evolution} showed that tag
similarity alone sustains cooperation without reciprocity,
and~\citet{roberts2002does} that this rests on the rule that identical tags
always donate, since cooperation collapses once agents may decline, which the
original authors disputed~\citep{riolo2002similarity}.
\citet{jansen2006altruism} showed that a continuously mutating tag space
outruns impostors, and~\citet{gardner2010greenbeards} reviewed the empirical
cases, which are real but
rare~\citep{keller1998selfish,queller2003single}. In biology forgeability is a
fact about the organism; in an agent economy it is a fact about the
verification
infrastructure~\citep{jovanovic2024watermarkstealing,anbiaee2026threatmodeling}.
The closest structural ancestor
is~\citet{hammond2006ethnocentrism}, whose agents carry a tag and two
conditional strategies, our design triple minus the badge type. Their tags
cannot be faked, so our question does not arise:
their result explains why differential treatment emerges, ours why it cannot
be removed from a certification regime without removing the regime.
\citet{choi2007parochial} pair in-group generosity with out-group hostility
too, but need group selection and explicit intergroup warfare,
while~\citet{masuda2015ingroup} give the standard condition that tag and
strategy be inherited as one unit, satisfied here by construction. Nearest to
the mechanism is tag-based or tolerance-based expulsion of those who do not
carry the mark~\citep{zhang2023expulsion,ren2021tolerance}, where exclusion is
a rule imposed on a spatial game; here it is one of four conduct options
towards unverified partners, and the mark survives forgery only under the harsh
ones. We need neither group selection nor a spatial substrate. Out-group
harshness is favoured because it is the only thing that makes a costly
membership signal worth acquiring honestly, so excludability, not conflict,
carries the result.

\paragraph{Signalling, certification and enforcement.}
Our object is Robson's secret handshake~\citep{robson1990efficiency}, a
pre-play signal that a mutant can display without the conduct it advertises;
what a forgeable badge adds is a display that fails at a rate the modeller
sets, turning a known vulnerability into a threshold in the pass rate. In the
handicap tradition signals are honest because they are differentially
costly to fake~\citep{zahavi1975mate,grafen1990biological}, and market
signalling separates types for the same reason~\citep{spence1973job}. Our
forger pays less than the compliant member, not more, and honesty need not rest
on a realised equilibrium cost~\citep{szamado2011cost}: here it rests on
detection. The setting is
nearer a market degraded by failed quality
signalling~\citep{akerlof1970market} and certification
intermediaries~\citep{lizzeri1999information,dranove2010quality}; the attribute
our badge certifies is never learned even after the encounter, which makes the
badge a credence rather than an experience
good~\citep{darby1973free,dulleck2006doctors}. Penalty and detection are
classically substitutes~\citep{becker1968crime};
Proposition~\ref{prop:fines} states the limits of that substitution when
detection is technological rather than budgetary.

\paragraph{AI races and agent identity.}
The interaction layer is the reduced race of~\citet{domingos2026falling},
descended from~\citet{armstrong2016racing} and formalised evolutionarily
by~\citet{han2020regulate}, whose programme covers voluntary commitments,
heterogeneity, auditing and
trust~\citep{han2022voluntary,cimpeanu2022heterogeneous,bova2024vigilant,bashir2026trust}.
Those instruments act on the race's payoffs or on an external monitor; ours
acts on who is recognised as an insider, the only lever available where the
payoff levers have no purchase (Proposition~\ref{prop:reciprocity}). Applied work on agent
governance treats visibility and provenance as
prerequisites~\citep{chan2024visibility,chan2025infrastructure,shavit2023practices,kolt2025governing},
and~\citet{hammond2025multiagent} name selection pressures and multi-agent
security, under which impersonation sits, among their risk factors without
formalising either. Work on model-origin cues documents self-favouring
evaluation and identity-conditioned
interaction~\citep{panickssery2024llm,long2025mirror,wang2026outgroup}, and
outputs carry detectable idiosyncratic signatures~\citep{sun2025idiosyncrasies}
while models show no consistent
self-recognition~\citep{davidson2024self}: recognition is available but
imperfect. What that strand lacks, and we supply, is the population-level
consequence of recognition that is imperfect and worth faking.
