\begin{abstract}
Identity credentials make machine agents treat partners differently and
thereby act as forgeable greenbeards. We attach genuine, forged or absent
badges and badge-contingent conduct to a four-strategy race, producing 48
designs on a 47-dimensional simplex under replicator dynamics, with a
finite-population counterpart. Transversal eigenvalues give the main invasion
boundaries in closed form; numerical integration is used only for basin
structure. A reciprocating club withstands more than twice the forgery of an
unconditionally trusting club, while the protective fine diverges as forged
badges become undetectable. Verification cannot nucleate a costly club without
positive assortment. At the baseline, a behavioural mimic invades before an
exploiter, destroying attestation integrity while conduct remains safe;
stronger assortment excludes the exploiter but not the mimic. Replicator
trajectories from uniform starts split between disjoint certified and
uncertified safe rest faces, whereas targeted starts reach a numerically robust
unsafe mixed face outside that sample. Certification therefore lowers harm
under recurrent displacement but not within either dominant settled regime.
Its stability comes from harsh treatment of unverified partners: removing that
entry barrier also removes resistance to forgery. Forgeability thus turns
certification into a multistable nonlinear system whose observable conduct can
remain benign after its identity signal has collapsed.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item A forgeable identity tag induces multistability in a 48-strategy flow
\item Conditional cooperation more than doubles tolerance to credential forgery
\item No finite fine replaces detection as forgery becomes undetectable
\item Mimics hollow out credential integrity before observable conduct degrades
\item Certification buys robustness through an exclusionary entry barrier
\end{highlights}
