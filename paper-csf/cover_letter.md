*Chaos, Solitons & Fractals*

Dear Editors,

We wish to submit the enclosed manuscript, **"Forgeable greenbeards:
bistability and hollow collapse in the evolutionary dynamics of certified agent
populations"**, for consideration as a regular paper in *Chaos, Solitons &
Fractals*.

**What the paper does.** We study a 48-strategy replicator system on a
47-dimensional simplex, together with its finite-population counterpart, in
which each strategy carries an identity tag that is genuine, forged or absent
and conditions its conduct on whether a partner's tag survives verification. A
forged tag passes with probability sigma, so the tag is a greenbeard whose
reliability is set by an engineering parameter rather than by heredity. Every
qualitative transition we report is a transversal eigenvalue of the flow
crossing zero, which makes each critical value a simple root available in
closed form; numerical integration confirms the thresholds rather than
producing them.

**Why it belongs in this journal.** The results are results about a nonlinear
flow, not about a policy. The flow is bistable, with two attractors on disjoint
faces of the simplex and no mixed attractor between them, and it is the basin
structure rather than the equilibrium level that carries the paper's main
conclusion. Two of the transitions are unusual enough to be worth naming: the
locus along which a penalty holds the certified regime marginally stable has a
vertical asymptote, so no finite penalty substitutes for detection; and the two
invasion thresholds cross at a codimension-two point at which the system
exchanges which of its two failure modes it meets first, so improving the
interaction structure removes the visible failure mode and leaves the invisible
one. We also report a methodological trap of general interest for multistable
systems: every population average of a pairwise quantity is bilinear in the
state, so evaluating one at a basin-weighted mean state manufactures
interactions that no attractor contains. In our case that error turns an
ecosystem whose unsafe frequency is below 1e-21 into one reported at 0.379.

**Interdisciplinarity and accessibility.** The paper sits between evolutionary
game dynamics, the economics of certification and the emerging engineering of
machine-agent identity. It is written to be read by someone who knows
replicator dynamics and nothing about attestation: the interaction layer is a
four-strategy game given in full as a payoff matrix, the identity layer adds one
Bernoulli parameter, and every threshold is a ratio of payoff differences.

**Originality and availability.** The work is original, is not under
consideration elsewhere, and all authors approve the submission. The complete
replication package, including the code that regenerates every quoted scalar
and every figure, is openly available at
<https://github.com/trungkiet2005/greenbeard-tag>.
