\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A forgeable identity badge turns a four-strategy race into a 48-design
nonlinear population system. Its regime boundaries are governed by
transversal eigenvalues: reciprocity and liability determine when identity is
needed, assortment determines whether a costly club can form, and detection
sets a limit that no finite fine can overcome. The first invader at the
baseline is a behavioural mimic, so credential integrity collapses before
observable conduct deteriorates.

Globally, the replicator flow is multistable. Uniform starts split between
certified and uncertified safe rest faces, while targeted starts reveal a
locally robust unsafe mixed face outside that sample. Certification therefore
reduces harm under recurrent displacement but not within either dominant
settled regime. The out-group conduct that supplies this resilience also
penalises compliant unbadged entrants; making the club gentle removes both the
barrier and its evolutionary reason to exist. Certification design must
therefore monitor credential integrity, specify badge-contingent conduct and
report basin-dependent outcomes rather than a single averaged population.
